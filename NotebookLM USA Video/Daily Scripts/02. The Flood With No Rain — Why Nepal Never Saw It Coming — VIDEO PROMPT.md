## PART 1 — STEERING PROMPT

```
Create a documentary-style video overview from the supplied script covering ONLY the opening hook through the end of the chapter "Two Thousand Feet of Mountain". Stop at "The earth shook because the mountain fell."

NARRATOR: Confident, fast-moving, fact-driven documentary narrator for a US English-speaking audience. Authoritative and precise. Investigative broadcast documentary, never sentimental.

OPEN: Deliver the hook in the first 15 seconds as written — seismometers logging a shock at about 8:37 a.m. on 26 August 2026 and briefly calling it an earthquake; it was a mountain coming apart; no storm, no rain; within half an hour an 8-to-10-metre wall of water; more than 900 dead and over 4,700 missing. Then the promise: this film shows how a flood that size assembled out of a clear sky, and why none of the alarms built to prevent it could have gone off.

COVER, IN THIS ORDER:
1. The paradox: catastrophic Himalayan floods normally follow days of monsoon rain, which is what gives people downstream any warning at all. On 26 August there was no rain in the equation.
2. The geography: the Lhende Khola feeding the Trishuli feeding the Narayani; the highway from Kathmandu to the Chinese border at Rasuwagadhi; Rasuwagadhi as a customs post, trade artery and the overland gateway to Mount Kailash. Name the settlements destroyed — Timure, the Rasuwagadhi customs area, parts of Syafrubesi, Mailung, Shantibazaar, Betrawati.
3. THE KEY FACT OF THIS PART — the cross-border blind spot: the collapse happened about 20 km northeast of the crossing, on the Tibetan side, inside China. Even a perfect Nepali sensor network would have been watching the wrong side of a national frontier. Hazard in one jurisdiction, casualties in another.
4. The collapse itself: a mass of ice, rock and debris detaching in the Langtang Lirung area at roughly 5,200 metres, per a study commissioned by the Nepal Environment Society; press accounts describing a glacier block on the order of 2,000 feet across; millions of tonnes released from a height comparable to four Empire State Buildings stacked and dropped into a narrow gorge.
5. The mechanism: a debris flow needs no reservoir behind it — it generates its own flood wave through displacement and mass, moving like wet concrete with a river's momentum. The ice is the water; it just has not become water yet.
6. Close on the inversion: the USGS made the sequence explicit — the seismic event was a consequence of the glacial collapse and debris flow, not a quake that caused it. The mountain did not fall because the earth shook; the earth shook because the mountain fell.

PACING: ~6 minutes. Brisk. Short declarative sentences.

VISUALS: Himalayan terrain and the Langtang Lirung massif, river-corridor maps tracing Lhende–Trishuli–Narayani, the Nepal–Tibet border and Rasuwagadhi, before/after satellite comparisons of the destroyed settlements, a simple scale diagram of the released block, a seismogram. No depictions of victims and no graphic aftermath imagery.

DO NOT: discuss the GLOF/earthquake elimination, the downstream wave heights, the casualty breakdown, the barrier lake or the warning-system conclusion — those are Parts 2 and 3. Do not add facts not in the script.

## PART 2 — STEERING PROMPT

```
Create a documentary-style video overview from the supplied script covering ONLY the chapters "Not a GLOF, Not an Earthquake", "Thirty Minutes to Galchhi" and "Eighty-Five Americans". Begin at "Now the forensic part" and end at "…generated the fewest headlines."

NARRATOR: The same confident, fast, fact-driven voice. This is the forensic core — tone should be investigative and methodical, eliminating explanations one at a time.

OPEN: A self-contained hook in the first 15 seconds — scientists spent weeks ruling out the two causes everyone assumed, and what they were left with has no sensor pointed at it anywhere in the Himalaya. Promise the elimination, the thirty-minute wave, and the American toll.

COVER, IN THIS ORDER:
1. ELIMINATION ONE — earthquake: the USGS did record a magnitude 4.4 in Tibetan territory around 8:37 a.m.; Nepal's foreign minister Shishir Khanal noted it registered minutes before the flood was reported, raising the possibility it shook a loaded slope loose. Plausible, not established — and a 4.4 is small, the kind the Himalaya produces routinely without destroying a valley.
2. ELIMINATION TWO — glacial lake outburst flood: explain the classic GLOF mechanism (meltwater behind a loose moraine dam, dam fails, lake empties in minutes) and that it is the mechanism the region's entire mitigation programme is built around. Then the finding: teams could not find a lake. No existing glacial lake near the collapse site. No reservoir to burst.
3. WHAT IS LEFT, AND THE KEY FACT OF THE EPISODE: the water was likely stored inside and beneath the glacier itself, released when the ice failed. Draw the contrast hard — a glacial lake is visible, can be photographed from orbit, inventoried, measured, fitted with a sensor and a siren, even drained by engineers. Water held beneath ice has no shoreline, no gauge, no satellite signature. You cannot inventory reservoirs you cannot see.
4. The wave: 8 to 10 metres in places; roughly 9 metres inside 30 minutes at Galchhi; about 7 metres at Malekhu. Thirty minutes is the entire warning window, and only for places far enough downstream to have one — for Timure and the customs area, the first notification was the water.
5. The built-up valley: a steep corridor with reliable flow is exactly where hydropower goes. Roads, bridges, powerhouses and tunnels destroyed; the Kathmandu highway closed. More than 100 people believed trapped in a hydropower tunnel with the Nepal Army deployed — note the grim geometry that a tunnel is the safest place in an earthquake and the worst place when a debris flood reaches the portal.
6. The toll climbing as the terrain fought the counting: dozens, then around 160, then hundreds, with Chinese authorities reporting separately for Tibet; within about a week at least 919 confirmed dead and more than 4,700 recorded missing. Handle "missing" honestly — in a disaster that destroyed border posts, customs records and transient worker settlements in one hour, it is partly a count of the dead and partly a measure of lost paperwork.
7. The foreign toll: Nepal's NDRRMA reporting 517 foreign nationals out of contact; American figures moving from 47 to 63 to 65, with State Department spokesperson Tommy Pigott confirming at least 85 Americans still missing and nine evacuated, up from five. Then the explanation — Rasuwagadhi is the land route to Mount Kailash, a major pilgrimage destination for Hindus, Buddhists, Jains and Bon followers, and Syafrubesi is the gateway to the Langtang trekking circuit, in peak season. Finish by stating plainly that the dead were overwhelmingly Nepali — customs officers, teashop and hotel workers, drivers, porters, guides, hydropower labourers — and generated the fewest headlines.

PACING: ~6.5 minutes. Hold on the "water stored beneath the ice" reveal — it is the spine of the episode. Then accelerate.

VISUALS: A clean two-option elimination graphic for earthquake vs GLOF, a cutaway diagram of water held within and beneath a glacier versus a surface moraine-dammed lake, river gauge/hydrograph showing a 9-metre rise in 30 minutes, a corridor map with Galchhi and Malekhu marked, a flag/nationality breakdown panel. Treat casualties respectfully — no bodies, no recreations of drowning or entrapment.

DO NOT: re-explain the collapse or the seismic inversion from Part 1. Do not cover the barrier lake or the 25,000-lake conclusion — that is Part 3.

## PART 3 — STEERING PROMPT

```
Create a documentary-style video overview from the supplied script covering ONLY "The Lake That Formed Afterwards", "Twenty-Five Thousand Lakes and the Wrong Alarm" and the closing section. Begin at "Here is the fact that tells you how little control anyone had" and run to the end.

NARRATOR: The same confident, fact-driven voice, now building to an argument. Analytical and unflinching. This part has to land a thesis, not just recount events.

OPEN: A self-contained hook in the first 15 seconds — at a certain point the rescue had to stop, not because the search was finished, but because the mountain was not. Promise the compounding chain and the reason every alarm in the region was pointed the wrong way.

COVER, IN THIS ORDER:
1. Chinese authorities pausing the Tibet search because the risk of renewed flooding was too high, and why: the debris had dammed a river. At the confluence of the Tsogyal and Purupqiangzangbu rivers a barrier lake had formed, and authorities warned it was overflowing and could burst.
2. What a barrier lake is and why it is worse than an engineered dam: an unplanned heap of rock, mud and ice holding back water that keeps arriving. Engineered dams are built to spill safely when overtopped; a debris dam does the opposite — overtopping water cuts a notch, the notch deepens, the channel widens, and erosion accelerates. Overtopping is the beginning of the collapse, not a release valve.
3. The chain, stated plainly: a mountain collapsed; a flood killed hundreds in a corridor with no warning; the debris built a new reservoir upstream; rescuers had to withdraw from searching for the missing. Conventional disaster planning is written for an event — this was a chain.
4. The inventory: per Nepal's International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development there are now more than 25,000 glacial lakes across the Hindu Kush Himalaya, proliferating as ice retreats, with GLOFs becoming more common as a direct result. Credit the real work — lakes are mapped, risk-ranked, monitored, and in the worst cases physically lowered.
5. THE THESIS — the system runs on two signals, rainfall and glacial lake levels, and on 26 August neither was present. No rain, no lake. The warning architecture was not overwhelmed or ignored; it was pointed somewhere else, watching for a catastrophe that did not happen while the one that did assembled where no instrument was looking.
6. Sharpen it: both working signals measure water — rain falling, water pooling. Neither measures the stability of frozen rock and ice at 5,200 metres, which is the variable that changes as a range warms and the variable that decided this. The region is instrumented to watch the symptom and blind to the cause. Warming is producing a different kind of danger than the one the defences were built against.
7. The close: recap the chain in one breath — a flood with no rain, from a lake that did not exist, triggered by a collapse that registered as an earthquake; 900-plus dead; 85 Americans off the map in one valley; border towns gone; a new reservoir left upstream. State that the barrier lake is still sitting there.
8. THE OPEN LOOP — tease the next episode: the ranked inventories scientists and agencies maintain of which Himalayan lakes and valleys are most dangerous right now, which have populations and hydropower beneath them, and which have no monitoring at all — some with towns of tens of thousands downstream. Next time, that list, valley by valley. Finish on the subscribe line.

PACING: ~6.5 minutes. Let the thesis in points 5 and 6 breathe — slow down there. Then drive hard into the open loop.

VISUALS: Barrier-lake formation and overtopping-erosion diagram, a map or dot-density graphic conveying 25,000 glacial lakes across the Hindu Kush Himalaya, a simple "what we monitor vs what actually failed" comparison panel, glacier retreat time-series imagery.

DO NOT: re-explain the collapse mechanics or the casualty figures in detail. Do not end without the open loop. Do not soften the thesis into a generic climate-change message — the specific point is a monitoring mismatch.
```
